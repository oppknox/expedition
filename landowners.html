Expedition 40

If this land is yours—or you steward it—this page is the whole ask in one place. Ten people. Ten days. September 2027. Washington State or coastal western Canada. Legal, temporary use. Then we restore the site and leave.

Entry L01 // How The Land Would Be Used

A short, permitted stay. Not a claim.

Expedition 40 is a private ten-day wilderness trip planned for September 2027 in Washington State or coastal western Canada. The group is small: about ten adults. The work on the land is camp work— primitive shelter-build, securing water, and fishing. We are asking for legal temporary use only. We are not asking to own the land, occupy it, or keep a standing right to return.

When

September 2027

Ten days in the field. The exact dates stay unset until a site is secured and the owner’s written permission is in hand.

Where

Washington or coastal western Canada

Remote enough to feel like wilderness. Candidate country includes remote Washington, Vancouver Island, coastal British Columbia, private islands, and legally authorized lake or shoreline land.

Who

About ten people

A small group of friends. Not a public event, not a festival, not spectators. Everyone in the party camps, works, and leaves together.

Shelter

Primitive, temporary builds

Teams would build primitive shelters from materials the owner allows. These are field structures for the ten days—not cabins, not foundations, not a lodge.

Water & food

Water, then fishing

Secure drinking water. Fish where it is legal and the owner allows it. Fires for cooking and warmth. Anything beyond that—timber use, traps, hunting—only if the written permission says so.

The legal line

Temporary use. Not a claim.

We do not want title, a lease that outlives the trip, a road, or a standing camp. Permission would be for a defined stay. When the stay ends, so does the use.

We are looking for permission to visit, camp, and leave—not a permanent place on the map.
Entry L02 // The Restore-On-Packout Promise

When we leave, we put it back.

We restore the site to the best of our ability when we leave. That is not a courtesy written for this page. It is already a rule of the expedition.

Leave the site better than we found it.
The site is restored and every temporary structure is dismantled unless the property owner directs otherwise.
Fire rings, cordage, caches, trash, and anything we packed in come out with us.
We do not leave a camp standing for next year.
If you want a structure left, you say so in writing. Otherwise it comes down.
Cleanup is part of packout, not an optional last chore.
Restore on packout: we leave the land as we found it—or better—to the best of our ability.

Ten people will have been there. We cannot honestly promise the ground will look untouched. We can promise the work of putting it back, and that we will not treat that work as optional.

Entry L03 // What This Page Is—And Is Not

Intent, stated plainly.

The site is not secured yet. Rights to camp, build primitive shelters, take water, and fish still have to be negotiated for a specific property, in writing. This page is so a landowner can see the plan and the promise without wading through the rest of the expedition.

This is not a contract, a permit, or a payment offer. Nothing here binds you. Nothing here lets us occupy land. If you are reading this because someone asked about your property, the facts are above. Questions can go to join@expedition40.com.
